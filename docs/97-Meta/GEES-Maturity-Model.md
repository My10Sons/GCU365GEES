# GEES Engineering Maturity Model

---

# Executive Summary

The GEES Engineering Maturity Model (GEMM) defines the maturity levels used to evaluate engineering practices, engineering artifacts, engineering disciplines, engineering projects, and organizational engineering capability.

The purpose is continuous engineering improvement through objective measurement.

---

# Purpose

The maturity model enables organizations to:

- Measure engineering capability
- Benchmark teams
- Improve engineering quality
- Increase automation
- Improve AI adoption
- Reduce engineering risk
- Improve predictability

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Engineering Organizations
- Engineering Teams
- Projects
- Products
- Knowledge Areas
- Engineering Artifacts
- Engineering Processes
- AI Engineering

---

# Maturity Principles

Engineering maturity SHALL be:

- Measurable
- Repeatable
- Auditable
- Evidence Based
- Continuously Improved

---

# Maturity Levels

## Level 1 — Initial

Characteristics

- Ad hoc engineering
- Limited documentation
- Individual practices
- Reactive management
- Minimal governance

Typical Indicators

- Missing standards
- Low traceability
- Manual processes
- Knowledge in people's heads

---

## Level 2 — Managed

Characteristics

- Documented processes
- Repeatable activities
- Basic governance
- Version control
- Defined ownership

Indicators

- Standards exist
- Reviews performed
- Documentation maintained

---

## Level 3 — Defined

Characteristics

- Enterprise-wide engineering standards
- Standardized engineering artifacts
- Complete traceability
- Cross-team consistency

Indicators

- Artifact compliance
- Engineering audits
- Engineering metrics

---

## Level 4 — Quantitatively Managed

Characteristics

- Engineering KPIs
- Automation
- AI assistance
- Continuous measurement
- Predictive analytics

Indicators

- Build quality
- Defect trends
- Traceability coverage
- Test coverage
- Automation coverage

---

## Level 5 — Adaptive Enterprise

Characteristics

- AI-driven engineering
- Continuous optimization
- Self-improving processes
- Knowledge graph driven
- Engineering intelligence

Indicators

- AI-assisted engineering
- Automated governance
- Continuous architecture validation
- Predictive quality analysis

# Assessment Process

Every assessment SHALL include:

1. Evidence Collection

2. Artifact Review

3. Compliance Verification

4. Metric Calculation

5. Improvement Recommendations

---

# Evidence Types

Accepted evidence includes:

- Documents
- Repository Metrics
- Test Reports
- Pipeline Results
- Audit Reports
- Review Records
- Architecture Reviews

---

# Maturity Scoring

Each dimension SHALL be scored.

Example

| Score | Maturity |
|--------|-----------|
|0–20|Level 1|
|21–40|Level 2|
|41–60|Level 3|
|61–80|Level 4|
|81–100|Level 5|

Organizations MAY customize scoring weights.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Assessment results SHALL produce:

- Improvement Backlog
- Priority Actions
- Target Maturity
- Review Schedule

---

# AI Responsibilities

AI development agents SHALL:

- Assess artifact maturity.
- Recommend improvements.
- Detect missing governance.
- Identify compliance gaps.
- Never assign official maturity ratings without human approval.
